# Distributed Evolution: Dynamics & Incentives — Will the NetHackers Model Actually Work?

- **Date:** 2026-08-09
- **Status:** Independent pre-commitment analysis (dynamics + incentives; analysis, not solutions)
- **Question:** Does "a distributed community collectively solving NetHack via narrow specialization + a shared gene pool + self-reported evidence + leaderboard/impact incentives" actually work as (a) an optimization process and (b) an incentive system? Where does it fail?
- **Scope note:** This report deliberately re-derives the dynamics from outside prior art rather than from the design spec's own framing. It takes as given: ~73 character identities, fitness = calibrated progression, north star = ascension, contributors run local MAP-Elites-style searches, hub = gene pool + identity×progress map + leaderboards + lineage impact, migration = pull elite → re-evaluate on own objective, evidence self-reported first / verified later.
- **Related:** [`2026-08-08-map-elites-objectives-analysis.md`](2026-08-08-map-elites-objectives-analysis.md) (Option D structure), [`2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md`](2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md) (domain facts), [`../specs/2026-08-08-nethackers-design.md`](../specs/2026-08-08-nethackers-design.md).

## 0. Executive summary

**Overall verdict: the optimization architecture is sound and well-precedented; the sociology and the evidence economics are where it breaks.** The system is a legitimate multi-task island model with a safe migration operator (MT-ME-style import-and-re-evaluate), operating on a substrate (deterministic symbolic programs over one shared root) where cross-task transfer is unusually cheap and real. But four load-bearing claims of the discussed model are weaker than assumed:

1. **"The community fills the map" is mostly false as stated — and mostly unnecessary.** Because one program plays all 73 identities, the map's low rungs are filled *mechanically* by any decent generalist plus one full-catalog evaluation — no crowd needed. What the crowd is actually needed for — hard-row frontiers (Wizard/Tourist/Healer) and the late-game columns (past Castle) — is exactly what volunteer-preference data (OpenStreetMap, iNaturalist, Zooniverse, TNNT participation) says volunteers do *not* do by default. Coverage-by-crowd is the wrong mental model; the map is better understood as "2–3 generalist lineages + a thin fringe of specialist deltas."
2. **The late game is not a neglected niche; it is a disconnected one.** Under episode-from-turn-0 evaluation with mean-progression fitness, milestones past the Castle contribute ~zero gradient (deaths concentrate early even for expert humans; AutoAscend's mean ending depth ≈ 3). No incentive fixes this, because no contributor *can* productively work on Gehennom/Planes code today — there is no mechanism (deterministic prefix bootstrapping, staged objectives, late-game seed states) to make the late game an addressable optimization target. This is the single biggest gap between the model and its north star.
3. **The trust ladder verifies honesty, not generality — and the search loop optimizes the wrong tier.** Tier-2 replay uses the *same public seeds* as tier-1, so the entire community search process optimizes public-seed progression; only rationed tier-3 measures the real objective. With small public seed sets, deterministic environments, and code-level genomes, seed-specific overfitting (up to literal per-seed action memorization) is the *default outcome* of good-faith optimization, not an attack (CoinRun: substantial overfit below ~4,000 training levels; Kaggle/Ladder: adaptive leaderboard overfitting is systematic). As specced, tier-2 also verifies score-given-trace, not trace-given-program.
4. **The incentive stack is directionally right (many small boards, firsts, tier-labeled evidence — all community-validated patterns) but the specific instruments have known exploits:** first-mover bulk sweeps of "firsts" by generalist runs, elite-laundering via minimal diffs, self-declared-influence disputes (citation-gaming analog), sybil-cheap GitHub identities, and — the moment virality actually works — a Hacktoberfest-style flood of low-quality registrations that lands exactly when verification capacity is smallest.

**Regime honesty:** base rates (TNNT ≈ 335 players/yr; Foldit: a few hundred active from ~10⁵ registered; Zooniverse Gini 0.54–0.94, ~⅔ of volunteers contribute once and leave) say the realistic sustained contributor count is single digits to low tens with power-law effort. At N≈5, incentive gaming is irrelevant and "community coverage" is fiction — the binding constraints are per-contributor compute/LLM budget, retention of the core few, and the late-game mechanism. At N≈500 (virality success), every gaming/verification concern activates within weeks. The design must be evaluated in both regimes; most of its incentive machinery only matters in the second, while most of its actual near-term risk lives in the first.

What genuinely works in the design: determinism makes verification radically cheaper than BOINC-style redundancy; the identity-partitioned map is a real anti-convergence structure; per-objective boards + firsts + coverage scores mirror what 20 years of NetHack tournaments converged on; import-and-re-evaluate is the theoretically correct migration operator. The model is worth building — but with eyes open that (i) its QD/illumination guarantees do not survive popularity-weighted human parent selection, (ii) the north star needs a dedicated late-game mechanism that does not yet exist in the design, and (iii) M2a's self-reported-then-verify posture is safe only while the project is small and obscure, which is in tension with "viral."

## 1. The system as a formal object

Strip the branding and the discussed model is:

- **A multi-population evolutionary algorithm** where each contributor run is a semi-isolated island with private selection pressure (their objective + local MAP-Elites archive), *volunteer-controlled* population sizing, lifetime, and task choice.
- **A star-topology migration medium** (the hub) carrying *only elites* (contributors register cell-winners, not populations), with **pull-based, re-evaluated migration** (import → re-score on the active objective before it can parent).
- **A shared-genome substrate**: every solution is a patch lineage over one AutoAscend root; one program plays every identity; "transfer" is therefore adoption or merge of code, not gene exchange.
- **A noisy, tiered evidence economy**: tier-1 self-reported on public seeds → tier-2 deterministic replay of the same seeds → tier-3 rationed held-out seeds; boards and the map are views over this evidence.
- **A reputation economy**: per-objective leaderboards, a 73×~10 coverage map, named "firsts," and a lineage-impact score (parents 1.0, influences 0.25, one split, no recursion), paid in status to volunteers whose dominant motivations — per the OSS motivation literature — are enjoyment, challenge, and community, not rankings ([Lakhani & Wolf 2003](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=443040)).

Four properties distinguish it from the systems it borrows credibility from (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, GigaEvo, BOINC, Kaggle):

1. **Nobody owns the compute.** FunSearch could reset its worst islands every 4 hours ([Romera-Paredes et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6)); AlphaEvolve's database allocates samples across islands ([DeepMind 2025](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/AlphaEvolve.pdf)); EC-Star's hub dispatched candidates to volunteer spokes ([O'Reilly, Wagy & Hodjat 2013](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4614-6846-2_6)). NetHackers' hub, by design, assigns nothing. Every allocation decision — which identity, which parent, how long to run — is a volunteer preference.
2. **Islands are ephemeral and elitist at the boundary.** Volunteer-computing EC literature treats node churn as the first-order design problem ([Laredo et al. 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10710-014-9213-5)); here a "node" is a person's background loop on a laptop. When a run dies, its archive — the stepping stones, the mediocre-but-novel cells — dies with it; only registered elites survive into the commons.
3. **The genome is code with an LLM mutation operator**, which imports the specification-gaming literature ([Krakovna et al.](https://vkrakovna.wordpress.com/2018/04/02/specification-gaming-examples-in-ai/)) into the fitness function's threat model.
4. **The public archive doubles as the status ledger.** In Picbreeder — the closest existing relative (a community archive where users branch from others' published artifacts) — the archive carried *no* competitive ranking, and its discoveries demonstrably came from non-objective, interest-driven branching ([Secretan et al. 2011](https://direct.mit.edu/evco/article/19/3/373/1371/Picbreeder-A-Case-Study-in-Collaborative); [Woolley & Stanley 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.6682); [Stanley & Lehman 2015](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-15524-1)). NetHackers overlays leaderboards on the same structure. That coupling is the source of most incentive pathologies below.

## 2. Q1 — Optimization dynamics: does decentralized specialization + migration cover the space?

### 2.1 What the theory actually promises, and on what conditions

The island model's benefits are real but conditional. Migration between semi-isolated populations provably beats both extremes — no-migration islands and one panmictic population — on problems needing both exploration and recombination of building blocks ([Whitley, Rana & Heckendorn 1999](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2244494_The_Island_Model_Genetic_Algorithm_On_Separability_Population_Size_and_Convergence); runtime analyses in [Lässig & Sudholt 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00500-013-0991-0) construct problems where the island model with migration succeeds in polynomial time and both extremes need exponential time). The conditions: migration must be **infrequent and small** relative to local search, or the system collapses to effective panmixia and a single lineage takes over.

The human-collective literature says the same thing more forcefully, because humans copy winners far more aggressively than a migration operator does:

- [Lazer & Friedman (2007)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2189/asqu.52.4.667): in networked collective problem solving, *more efficient* information diffusion improves short-run performance and *reduces* long-run performance — well-connected groups converge on good-not-great solutions; poorly-connected groups keep exploring and end higher.
- [Derex & Boyd (2016, PNAS)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1518798113): in a lab cultural-evolution task, **0% of fully-connected groups** discovered the most complex innovations vs **58% of partially-connected groups** — copying the successful destroys the solution diversity that complex discoveries require.
- [Lorenz et al. (2011, PNAS)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108): even mild social information (seeing others' answers) collapses estimate diversity and undermines crowd accuracy.
- The empirical human version of "hot migration": Kaggle's public-notebook economy, where high-scoring solutions are "forks of forks of forks" and openness produces measurable homogenization of approaches ([Kaggle forum on public-kernel dynamics](https://www.kaggle.com/discussions/general/157149); [Kaggle Chronicles, arXiv 2511.06304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06304)).

**Implication for NetHackers:** the *formal* topology (each run an island, pull migration) looks conservative, but the *effective* topology is a star around a public leaderboard whose global best is maximally salient. The social copying channel — "rebase my harness on the #1 elite because it's #1" — is not governed by the every-3-generations import cadence; it is governed by attention. Expect effective migration pressure far above what island-model theory recommends, i.e., a strong pull toward one dominant lineage per popular objective. Two design features genuinely push back: the identity-partitioned map (73 niches whose scores never compete, so there is no single "global #1" to collapse onto — the same logic as TNNT's abandonment of unified scoring) and mandatory local re-evaluation (imports must survive your objective). One design feature makes it worse: lineage-impact credit rewards being ancestor to others' work, which rewards making your elite the thing everyone rebases on.

### 2.2 QD's coverage guarantee does not survive human parent selection

MAP-Elites' illumination property — the map fills, including unfashionable regions — rests on **uniform-ish random selection across archive cells** ([Mouret & Clune 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909)): every niche keeps getting parented, so every niche keeps improving, and stepping stones survive in cells nobody "wants." In the distributed system, cell-selection probability is the community's attention distribution — and attention in volunteer systems is power-law concentrated and preference-biased:

- iNaturalist observers disproportionately record conspicuous, charismatic taxa; taxonomic bias is pervasive and tracks societal preference, not scientific need ([Geurts et al. 2023](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecs2.4582); [Troudet et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-09084-6)).
- OpenStreetMap building completeness exceeds 80% for 16% of the world's urban population and is under 20% for 48% — coverage tracks where mappers live and like to map; the documented counter-force was *explicit humanitarian task routing*, not leaderboards ([Herfort et al. 2023, Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39698-6)).
- Zooniverse: top 10% of volunteers contribute ~79% of work in the most unequal projects, Gini 0.54–0.94, and roughly two-thirds of volunteers make a single contribution and never return ([Spiers et al. 2023, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0293289); [Patterns of Volunteer Behaviour, WWW'18](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3184558.3186945)).
- NetHack's own community: bots and novices alike concentrate on Valkyrie-class roles; identity-completion (Z-score, Great Race/Role) is a *veteran minority* pursuit ([role-difficulty data and TNNT participation in the community-insights report](2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md)).

So the honest model of the distributed archive is: **a MAP-Elites archive whose parent-selection distribution is proportional to niche popularity**. That is much closer to "a few parallel hill-climbers on the popular niches, plus occasional cross-niche adoption" than to illumination. The local (within-run) MAP-Elites layers retain real QD behavior — but they are private and die with the run (§2.4).

**The saving grace — and it is large:** because programs are character-agnostic and evaluation is per-identity, *popularity bias in effort does not translate into popularity bias in coverage at the current frontier*. One generalist improvement, canonically evaluated, advances all 73 rows at once. Coverage of the map's reachable region is nearly free; what popularity bias actually starves is (a) role-conditional code for the structurally weak rows (spellcasting for Wizard, protection-racket/stethoscope logic for Healer, camera/darts logic for Tourist — precisely AutoAscend's documented gaps) and (b) verification/eval compute for unfashionable objectives. Piling-on is real, but it manifests as *frontier stagnation on hard rows*, not as empty left-half cells.

### 2.3 Two coverages: rows fill mechanically; frontiers need directed effort

Decompose "does the map get covered":

| Region of the map | How it fills | Bottleneck | Prognosis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Left/low rungs, all 73 rows | Any generalist × one full-catalog eval | Someone must *run and pay for* canonical 73-identity evaluations (up to ~30 min/episode at challenge horizons — hours per candidate) | Fills early; produces an impressive-looking coverage % that is really a picture of 1–3 generalist lineages |
| Frontier of easy rows (Val/Bar/Sam/Mon) | Popular; leaderboard-salient; where the fitness gradient lives | LLM/eval budget | Overserved; likely monoculture (§2.1) |
| Frontier of hard rows (Wiz/Tou/Hea/Pri) | Requires deliberate specialist objectives + role-conditional code | Volunteer preference; weaker gradient (lower baseline, more variance); *and* these fixes only partially transfer back | Underserved by default; this is where incentive design must actually bite (§4.6) |
| Right columns (Castle → Ascension), all rows | Currently: nothing reaches them | **No gradient and no addressable substrate** (§2.5) | Will not fill from leaderboard pressure at all |

The middle two rows of this table are incentive problems. The last row is not — it is an algorithmic gap.

### 2.4 Ephemeral islands and an elitist commons: where stepping stones go to die

QD's core argument is that current-mediocre, behaviorally-novel candidates are the ancestors of later champions ([Pugh, Soros & Stanley 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2016.00040/full)); Picbreeder demonstrated the same for human-collective search — the stepping stones to a discovery do not resemble the discovery ([Woolley & Stanley 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.6682)). In NetHackers, stepping-stone preservation happens *only inside contributors' local archives*, because the publish rule is "register if a new/improved elite." The hub commons therefore accumulates winners and structurally filters out the diversity that made them. Combined with volunteer churn (two-thirds one-and-done; laptops close; runs end), the system-level memory is: **elites persist, exploration state evaporates**. FunSearch and AlphaEvolve kept whole island populations precisely because resampling non-best programs mattered; the closest NetHackers equivalent — encouraging registration of interesting non-elites (unique deaths, novel behaviors) — exists only as a diagnostic board ("best YASD"), not as a gene-pool input. Expect the community search to be more exploitative and more path-dependent than the local-MAP-Elites framing suggests.

### 2.5 The late game: a disconnected component, not a hard niche

The design's own domain research is unambiguous: games are decided early (expert humans: "hardly anybody dies after the Castle" — [codehappy expert data](https://codehappy.net/nethack/data.htm)); AutoAscend's mean ending depth is ≈ 3.1 with bimodal camping/diving ([NetHack is Hard to Hack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19240)); no NeurIPS-2021 entrant came near ascension in >500k games ([Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)). Mean-progression fitness therefore concentrates essentially all gradient in Dlvl 1–15 — correctly, per the calibration. But this has a consequence the discussed model does not confront:

- A contributor cannot *work on* Gehennom, the Planes, or the ascension run. There is no evaluation that exercises that code: episodes start at turn 0, the bot dies at Dlvl ~3–11, and a hypothetical perfect Planes module changes measured fitness by ~0.
- This is precisely the **detachment/derailment** structure that defeats naive exploration in hard-exploration RL: the algorithm never returns to the deep states it occasionally reaches, so it can never practice from them. The known remedy is explicit state-anchoring — "first return, then explore," solve deterministically then robustify ([Ecoffet et al., Go-Explore, Nature 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-03157-9)). NetHackers is *unusually well-positioned* for this — deterministic seeded episodes mean any recorded run to the Castle is a replayable springboard (replay the action prefix, hand control to candidate late-game code) — but nothing in the model (objectives, fitness, evidence formats, boards) represents "performance from a deep prefix" today, and NLE has no native save/restore to shortcut the replay cost.
- The open-endedness literature adds a second warning: in POET, many hard environments were **only** solved by policies transferred in from other niches after indirect stepping-stone progress — direct optimization on the hard niche failed even with a curriculum ([Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)). Translated: "first Gehennom entry" will not come from someone grinding a Gehennom objective; it will come from mid-game robustness compounding until deep arrivals are common enough to iterate on — *if* the system can then convert rare deep arrivals into addressable test states.

**Conclusion for Q1:** decentralized specialization + pull migration will (a) advance the generalist frontier briskly at first, (b) cover the map's reachable region essentially as a side effect, (c) converge toward few dominant lineages per popular niche unless niche-partitioned credit keeps specialists distinct, (d) underserve hard-row specialist code — an incentive-shaped gap — and (e) make no contact with the late game — an algorithm-shaped gap that incentives cannot close. As an optimization process for "maximize map area under the current fitness," it works. As a process pointed at *ascension*, it is missing its last mechanism.

### 2.6 Noise and churn at the cell level (minor but real)

Strict-improvement replacement on 2-episode screens means cells are claimed and defended on high-variance estimates; the hub map at least sits on larger verified suites, but local archives will churn, and self-reported map cells will encode luck. This is a known noisy-QD issue with known mitigations (re-evaluation of elites, larger confirmation budgets) and is flagged in the MAP-Elites report; it matters here mainly because *public credit* (cells, firsts) attaches to what may be seed-lottery outcomes — an incentive irritant more than an optimization one.

## 3. Q2 — The transfer assumption: does a good idea for one identity help another?

### 3.1 The case for (strong, and structural)

1. **Interface fact:** one program plays every identity; a candidate is a single codebase whose behavior conditions on observed stats/inventory. Any improvement to shared logic — crash fixes, map/menu parsing, food/corpse safety, Elbereth/prayer discipline, Sokoban solving, item identification — is *by construction* an improvement for all 73 rows pending re-measurement. This is not a hopeful analogy to multi-task transfer; it is literally the same artifact.
2. **The killers are shared.** The NAO top-25 causes of death are role-agnostic early-game trash (jackals, dwarves, soldier ants, fountains, rotten corpses) ([NAO top deaths](https://www.alt.org/nethack/topdeaths.html)); a YASD defense written for anyone protects everyone.
3. **Empirical role correlation:** in the NeurIPS challenge, *all* agents — symbolic and neural — ranked roles nearly identically (best: Bar/Mon/Sam/Val; worst: Hea/Rog/Tou/Wiz) ([Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)). Shared competence dominates role-specific competence in the current regime.
4. **Formal precedent:** Multi-task MAP-Elites — competition per task, selection across tasks — beats independent per-task optimization at equal budget when tasks are related ("similar tasks have similar solutions"), on thousands of tasks ([Mouret & Maguire 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04407); [Anne & Mouret 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01264)). POET's cross-niche transfer was often the *only* route to hard niches ([Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)).
5. **Human-collective precedent:** Foldit players' shared "recipes" — 5,400+ strategy scripts created, modified, recombined, and spread through the population — collectively rediscovered (and, within game constraints, outperformed) an unpublished lab algorithm ([Cooper et al. 2010, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09304); [Khatib et al. 2011, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1115898108)). Shared executable know-how compounding across a hobbyist community is proven.

### 3.2 The case against (real, and specific)

1. **Transfer decays exactly where the map needs it.** The identities that lag do so because they need *divergent* competencies: real spellcasting (Wizard), protection-racket and stethoscope play (Healer), darts/camera/late-blooming builds (Tourist), steed logic (Knight). This is the low-task-similarity regime where the evolutionary-multitasking literature documents **negative transfer** and requires actively throttling inter-task exchange ([MFEA-II, Bali et al.](https://memetic-computing.org/publication/journal/FinalSub.pdf); [survey evidence on negative transfer at low task similarity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6971124/)). Concretely: a spellcasting rewrite can regress melee rows (the design's own "specialist regressions" example), and a melee-tuned trunk adopted by a Wizard specialist erases their divergence. Import-and-re-evaluate correctly *detects* negative transfer (the import loses on your objective and is discarded — wasting only one evaluation); it does not *create* positive transfer where none exists.
2. **The migration operator is adopt-or-discard, not merge.** "Pull elite, re-evaluate, use as parent" replaces or forks a lineage; it does not combine two lineages' independent gains. Once two forks diverge substantially (a Wizard fork with 40 spell-logic commits vs a generalist trunk with 30 robustness commits), capturing both requires a semantic code merge — an operator the model assumes the LLM mutator provides but nowhere validates. FunSearch's implicit crossover (prompting with k=2 programs) worked on ~10–40-line functions ([Nature 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6)); AutoAscend is a multi-thousand-line strategy DAG. The Netflix Prize is the cautionary human tale: the endgame was won by *teams merging* their divergent solutions, and the merge (blending) was itself the hard, decisive work ([Bell & Koren 2007](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1345448.1345465); [competition-then-cooperation dynamics](https://mat.tepper.cmu.edu/blog/?p=819)). Expect the same here: the gene pool's value compounds only if fork-merging is routine; otherwise the pool degenerates into parallel lineages that occasionally *replace* each other.
3. **Duplicate discovery is uncoordinated.** The hub deduplicates artifacts (digests), not ideas. Ten contributors will independently teach their forks the same floating-eye rule. In FunSearch/AlphaEvolve one operator amortizes this; in a volunteer star, redundancy is paid at every island. This is a real compute tax of decentralization — partially offset by fast idea diffusion through the pool, i.e., by exactly the herding force that threatens diversity (§2.1). Diversity and dedup trade off against each other through the same dial (how hot migration runs).

### 3.3 When import-and-re-evaluate pays, and when it wastes compute

| Situation | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold-starting any objective/row | **Strongly pays** | Adopt the best generalist; one eval buys the entire accumulated trunk. The single highest-ROI action in the system. |
| Weak-row specialist importing generalist trunk gains | Pays if merged; risky if adopted wholesale | Adoption erases specialist divergence; needs merge (§3.2.2). |
| Frontier generalist importing another generalist's near-duplicate | Mostly waste | Near-zero information; herding cost; one eval each across the whole community. |
| Importing a *behaviorally distant* elite (e.g., a diver into a robustness lineage) | Pays in expectation | This is the QD complementary-parent pattern; the local kernel already implements it. |
| High-cadence imports (every few generations) | Net negative at community scale | Effective panmixia; the Lazer-Friedman/Derex-Boyd regime (§2.1). Cheap per-event, expensive in lost diversity. |
| Late-game module imports | Currently meaningless | No evaluation exercises them (§2.5). |

**Conclusion for Q2:** the transfer assumption is **well-founded for the shared trunk and the early/mid game** — better-founded than in almost any published multi-task EC setting, because the "tasks" share one executable artifact. The global gene pool is worth it. But its payoff profile is front-loaded (huge while the trunk dominates, tapering as rows specialize), it lacks a validated merge operator for divergent forks (the actual mechanism by which "a good idea for one identity helps another" once specialization begins), and hot migration converts its benefit into monoculture. "Transfer helps" is true; "therefore the pool needs no further design" is not.

## 4. Q3 — Incentives and mechanism design

### 4.1 Who shows up, and why (base rates first)

- **Motivation:** OSS contributors are driven primarily by enjoyment/challenge/learning, with status and career secondary ([Lakhani & Wolf 2003](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=443040)). The NetHack community's own status economy runs on public, replayable, *linkable* evidence and celebrated failure — an unusually good cultural fit for the design's shareable runs and YASD boards.
- **Participation shape:** power-law, high churn (§2.2 data; Foldit's active core is a few hundred players, newcomers rarely finish the tutorials — [UW/Foldit reporting](https://www.engr.washington.edu/news/trend/autumn-2010/fold-it-game), [Horizons 2018](https://www.horizons-mag.ch/2018/03/08/gamers-can-solve-science-problems/)). Plan for: a handful of heavy contributors, a long tail of one-shot drive-bys, and the heavy few doing ~80% of everything — including, implicitly, moderation and verification triage.
- **Warning from adjacent history:** attaching a cheap extrinsic reward to a low-cost public contribution action converts the long tail into spam. Hacktoberfest 2020 attached a T-shirt to "4 PRs" and open-source maintainers were flooded with junk within days (≥4% of PRs marked spam/invalid on day one; opt-in rules had to be invented mid-event) ([DevRel case study](https://developerrelations.com/case-studies/overcoming-hacktoberfest-spam/); [contemporaneous account](https://joel.net/how-one-guy-ruined-hacktoberfest2020-drama)). NetHackers' `register` is a low-cost public action; leaderboard placement and "impact" are its T-shirt.

### 4.2 Behavior-by-behavior analysis of the incentive stack

| # | Incentivized behavior | Mechanism that produces it | Prior art | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Piling on easy rows / popular objectives | Salience of top boards; gradient is strongest where baseline is strongest; volunteers do what's fun | OSM/iNaturalist coverage bias; Kaggle competition concentration; TNNT most-ascensions focus | Medium — hurts hard-row frontier, not map area (§2.3) |
| 2 | Neglecting late game | No gradient, no credit reachable at all | Go-Explore detachment; POET (hard niches solved only via transfer) | **High — but it is not an incentive problem (§2.5)** |
| 3 | Overfitting public seeds | Tiers 1–2 and the entire local search loop score on the fixed public set | Kaggle public-LB overfitting & the Ladder ([Blum & Hardt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)); log-loss oracle attacks ([Whitehill 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01825)); CoinRun overfit <4k levels ([Cobbe et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02341)) | **High — corrupts the search signal itself (§4.3)** |
| 4 | Inflated/false self-reports | Tier-1 numbers are unverified at post time; boards display them | SWE-bench: 99/100 leaderboard entries self-reported, rankings acknowledged misleading ([2026 analysis](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/swe-bench-verified-june-2026-benchmark-vs-scaffolding-analysis)); BOINC forced into redundancy+quorum by credit cheating ([Anderson, BOINC history](https://continuum-hypothesis.com/boinc_history.php); [SETI transition](https://setiathome.berkeley.edu/transition.php)) | Medium at small N; high post-virality (§4.5) |
| 5 | Elite laundering (pull elite, trivial diff, register as own) | Cells/firsts credit the registering solution; parents declared by the child | Kaggle notebook-medal gaming & upvote rings ([hidden-rules thread](https://www.kaggle.com/discussions/product-feedback/425108)); citation self-inflation | Medium — corrodes credit legitimacy, not optimization |
| 6 | Under-declaring influences / credit disputes | `influences` are self-declared by the beneficiary; 0.25 weight taxes honesty | Citation gaming/cartels (30–80% of some authors' citations in extreme cases) ([NAS review](https://www.nas.org/academic-questions/34/1/citations-and-gamed-metrics-academic-integrity-lost); [J. Acad. Ethics 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10805-025-09631-1)) | Low-medium — social friction among the core few |
| 7 | Firsts-sweeping | A generalist canonical run claims *all* currently-reachable firsts across 73 rows at once | Analogous to airdrop-claim races; no direct precedent needed — it is arithmetic | Medium — one-time events that misallocate the scarcest credit (named firsts) away from specialist work |
| 8 | Strategic withholding of parents | One-split no-recursion impact: publishing early maximizes others' ability to out-credit you on your own lineage | Red Balloon: MIT paid *recursively up the chain* precisely to make contributing-to-others'-wins incentive-compatible ([Tang et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.3172); [verification study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0045924)) | Low-medium — counteracted by firsts/cell-claiming value of early publishing |
| 9 | Sybil/spam registration | GitHub accounts are cheap; register is cheap; boards/impact are the payoff | Gitcoin quadratic-funding sybil/collusion arms race ([BlockScience](https://blog.block.science/how-to-attack-and-defend-quadratic-funding/)); Hacktoberfest | Low pre-virality; at virality it is primarily a **verification-queue DoS** (§4.5) |
| 10 | LLM-mutator specification gaming | The mutator optimizes measured fitness; measured ≠ intended | Krakovna catalog; RL-trained models exploiting test harnesses ([Bondarenko et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13295)) | Medium — mostly indistinguishable from #3; occasionally exotic (exploiting `progress.py`/NLE quirks) |

Two non-problems worth naming, because they are often miscounted as risks:

- **Free-riding on others' elites is the mechanism, not an abuse.** Solutions are non-rival; every rebase spreads the trunk. The only genuine cost of free-riding is credit fairness (#5, #6) — an important distinction because "punishing free-riders" would attack the system's engine.
- **Score-lying at small N is nearly pointless.** Determinism + public artifacts + a community whose culture is built on replayable evidence (ttyrecs, dumplogs, xlogfiles) make fabrication low-payoff and high-embarrassment among the people who will actually be present in year one. The lying problem is a scale problem, not a day-one problem.

### 4.3 The Goodhart analysis of the metric + seeds (the deepest incentive hole)

Apply the [Manheim & Garrabrant taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585) to fitness = mean calibrated progression on a fixed public seed set:

- **Regressional:** top map cells and board ranks will systematically encode seed-luck (2-episode local screens; small public suites). Winner's-curse at every cell boundary.
- **Extremal:** the progression→ascension calibration is fit on *human* play distributions (BALROG on NLD-NAO). Bots occupy state regions humans never occupy — e.g., low-XL depth-diving — where "reached Dlvl 12" no longer carries its calibrated win-probability meaning. Mean-aggregation (chosen deliberately to reward the deep tail) sharpens this: a kamikaze diver can out-rank a consolidating survivor while being *further* from ascension-capable play. The mirror image of the 2021 challenge's score-camping pathology, on a better metric but the same failure class.
- **Causal:** milestone rungs are *markers* of ascension-capable play, not causes. Optimizing marker-reaching (get to Castle once, on any seed, in any condition) does not produce the thing the marker was calibrated against (arriving at Castle *with a kit*). The map's cell semantics ("best solution getting identity I to rung R") inherit this.
- **Adversarial/spec-gaming:** the sharpest version — with deterministic seeded episodes, a *public* seed list, and code as genome, the degenerate optimum of tiers 1–2 is a per-seed policy: branch on a seed fingerprint (initial glyphs/inventory identify the seed) and replay a stored strong trajectory. No malice required; an LLM mutator honestly maximizing the measured number will drift toward seed-specialization continuously (special-casing exact observed situations), with the lookup-table as the limit. CoinRun quantifies how fast this happens even for far less expressive policies ([Cobbe et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02341)); Kaggle history shows adaptive re-querying of a fixed holdout degrades it even when hidden ([Blum & Hardt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)).

Two structural facts make this worse than a normal Kaggle situation:

1. **Tier-2 verifies the wrong proposition.** Replay confirms score-given-actions on the same public seeds — it detects *misreporting*, not *overfitting* (overfit claims replay perfectly), and, as specced ("replay of the submitted actions, no program re-run"), it does not even confirm the submitted program *produced* those actions. A hand-played or per-seed-crafted trace attached to a trivial program passes tier-2. (Speedrunning communities learned this class of fraud the hard way; determinism makes the fix — re-running the program, which is exactly `eval` — cheap, but it is not what tier-2 currently is.)
2. **The search loop itself only sees public seeds.** Even a perfectly honest community produces a gene pool optimized for the public suite, because tier-3 is rationed and cannot drive anyone's local selection. The anti-overfitting instrument exists (held-out HMAC seeds — the right instrument) but sits outside the optimization loop; the public/private gap will be discovered at verification time as systematic "shakeup," Kaggle-style, including for the map's proudest cells.

This is the one place where the incentive design and the optimization design are the *same* problem: the leaderboard question ("what do boards rank on?") and the search question ("what does selection see?") share the seed-set-governance answer, and currently both point at the fixed public set.

### 4.4 Credit and lineage: legitimacy under dispute

The impact design (parents 1.0, influences 0.25, one split, no recursion) is admirably lean and avoids infinite-regress credit inflation — but note what it buys and what it costs:

- **It buys** resistance to deep self-citation chains and recursive sybil trees (each child splits credit once; no compounding).
- **It costs** the incentive that a shared-gene-pool system most needs: rewarding *being built upon*. Foundational trunk work is diluted linearly as lineages lengthen; the Red Balloon result is that paying up the chain, transparently and by formula, is what made "help the network win" individually rational ([Tang et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.3172)). A no-recursion rule plus self-declared influences leans on honesty exactly where academic citation practice — the closest long-running analog — demonstrates chronic under-attribution and organized gaming ([NAS review](https://www.nas.org/academic-questions/34/1/citations-and-gamed-metrics-academic-integrity-lost)).
- **Disputes are asymmetric to adjudicate.** Code provenance is unusually auditable here (diffs against a pinned root; content digests) — far better than most credit systems — but *influence* (I read your approach and re-implemented it) is exactly as unprovable as in academia. Expect the disputes to concentrate on influences and on firsts-timing, not on parents.
- **Evaluation-only contributions are unpriced.** Running someone else's solution on an unclaimed identity/suite creates real information (map cells, possibly firsts) at real compute cost, with zero authored code. The model currently either credits it as a "first" to the *solution author* (correct for coverage, demotivating for the evaluator) or invites minimal-diff laundering to make the evaluation registrable (#5). A credit category the design hasn't decided.

### 4.5 Scale discontinuity: verification capacity is the real attack surface

At small N, self-report + flag-on-divergence is fine (and the "divergence = bug or lie, we don't care" posture is genuinely good — blame-free, cheap, culturally aligned; its one caveat is that determinism engineering must be airtight or honest contributors get "liar" flags from environment drift). The discontinuity arrives with virality, and prior art is consistent about the shape:

- The flood arrives *at* the incentive moment, not gradually (Hacktoberfest day-one spam; airdrop-farming behavior in crypto grants ([Gitcoin governance forum](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/airdrop-farming/10938))).
- Every unverified claim is a demand on tier-2/3 workers — hub-side, operator-paid compute. BOINC could afford quorum validation because *volunteers* supplied the redundant compute; NetHackers' verification is centralized. Sybil-cheap registration + operator-paid verification = an asymmetric-cost DoS on the trust ladder, and a long unverified window during which boards and the map display whatever was claimed (the SWE-bench condition: a famous leaderboard where 99/100 entries are self-reported and the ranking is acknowledged to mislead ([2026 analysis](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/swe-bench-verified-june-2026-benchmark-vs-scaffolding-analysis))).
- Sybil identity cost is one GitHub account; the payoffs (board presence, firsts-squatting, influence-ring inflation of impact) all pay in the currency the project advertises. None of this is exotic; all of it is what the same incentives produced elsewhere.

### 4.6 What actually produces breadth and honest hard-niche effort (evidence, not designs)

The deliverable asks which mechanisms *genuinely* drive whole-map coverage. Ranked by strength of evidence:

1. **Named firsts / trophies for hard combos** — the NetHack community's own 20-year-tested motivator (devnull star ladder, TNNT Lesser/Great trophies, per-identity "NetHack Master"); status attaches to *named, specific, hard* achievements, and veterans structure their play around them. Strongest available lever, with the caveat that firsts must be protected from bulk generalist sweeps (§4.2.7) to retain meaning.
2. **Many small boards with diminishing-returns coverage scoring** — TNNT abandoned unified scoring as unfixably biased; Z-score (1, ½, ⅓… per repeated role) is the community's own anti-piling formula. Proven to engage the completionist minority — and it is a minority; do not expect it to move the median contributor.
3. **Explicit gap salience and (opt-in) task routing** — the only intervention with documented effect on coverage bias at scale: OSM's inequality receded where humanitarian mapping *tasked* specific areas ([Herfort et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39698-6)); Zooniverse routes tasks. The design's "empty cells are the roadmap" is the weak form; the strong form (a "most-wanted cells" surface, still pull-based) is compatible with the thin-hub principle and is where the evidence points.
4. **Verified-tier gating of all default surfaces** — Kaggle's public/private split and the Ladder exist because display-rank must not equal claimable-rank; the design already has tier filters; the question is only which tier the *default* views show.
5. **Targeted subsidy of hard niches** (bounties/prizes per cell or per first) — inducement prizes reliably elicit parallel effort (Netflix Prize), but bounty history also shows effort flowing to the bounty, not the goal, and gaming at the margins; a weaker, later lever.
6. **Spectacle and failure-celebration** (death announcer, best-YASD, streamed runs) — retention infrastructure, per the community's own culture; indirectly serves coverage by keeping the veteran core (who do the hard rows) engaged.

**Conclusion for Q3:** the incentive stack as discussed is *culturally* right (it copies the community's own institutions) but *mechanically* incomplete: it under-specifies seed governance (the Goodhart hole), verification economics at scale, firsts semantics under generalist sweeps, and the credit status of evaluation-only and influence-only contributions. None of these are fatal; all of them are cheaper to decide before the ledger is public than after (credit systems are the hardest thing to change retroactively — every change re-litigates the past).

## 5. FINAL — Audit of the discussion's assumptions

### A1. "The community collectively fills the map"

**Verdict: holds in the letter, fails in the spirit — and the spirit is what matters.** The map's area fills without any community at all: one generalist lineage + canonical full-catalog evaluation populates every row up to the current frontier (the character-agnostic interface guarantees this; it is the design's own §4 "key property"). What needs a community — hard-row specialist code and frontier pushes — is what volunteer-preference base rates (OSM, iNaturalist, Zooniverse, TNNT) predict will *not* happen by default, and what the realistic contributor count (single digits to low tens, power-law effort) cannot brute-force. Restate the assumption honestly: *"a few generalist lineages fill the map; a handful of specialists, if retained and pointed at gaps, advance the hard rows; the late columns don't move at all under the current mechanism."* Also unpriced: canonical full-catalog evaluation is hours of compute per candidate — *someone* must run it or the map is stale self-reported patchwork.

### A2. "Transfer helps → a global gene pool is worth it"

**Verdict: holds — the best-supported assumption of the four.** Structural (one artifact plays all identities), empirical (role-performance correlation across all 2021 entrants; shared early-game killers), formal (MT-ME at equal budget), and human-collective (Foldit recipes) evidence all agree. Three qualifications: (i) transfer weakens exactly on the hard rows that need divergent competencies — negative-transfer regime, correctly *detected* but not *solved* by import-and-re-evaluate; (ii) the pool's compounding depends on a fork-*merge* operator that is assumed (LLM crossover) but unvalidated at AutoAscend scale — without it the pool converges to adopt-or-replace dynamics; (iii) hot migration + leaderboard salience converts the pool's benefit into monoculture (Lazer-Friedman/Derex-Boyd/Kaggle-forks regime). "Worth it" — yes; "self-regulating" — no.

### A3. "A coverage board incentivizes breadth (covering hard identities)"

**Verdict: shaky.** Coverage scoreboards move the completionist minority (real, but small — the Z-score/Great-Race persona), while the median contributor follows salience and gradient to the easy frontier. Worse, a coverage % metric will look excellent early (mechanical fill, §A1) and stall precisely when breadth gets hard — rewarding the system for the part that was free. The evidence-backed levers for hard-row effort are named firsts/trophies (protected from bulk sweeps), diminishing-returns scoring, and explicit gap salience/opt-in routing (the only intervention with documented coverage effects at scale, per OSM). A board alone is a display, not an incentive.

### A4. "Self-reported-then-verify is enough for M2a"

**Verdict: conditionally holds — for a small, obscure, good-faith community, which is the antithesis of the project's viral ambition.** Three separate holes, in increasing order of importance: (i) *authorship*: tier-2 as specced (replay actions, don't re-run the program) cannot tell a bot's trace from a human's or a crafted one — determinism makes the stronger check cheap, but it isn't the current tier-2; (ii) *window*: between claim and verification, boards and map display fiction — tolerable at N≈10 claims/week, SWE-bench-condition at scale, and the flood historically arrives on day one of the incentive, not gradually (Hacktoberfest); verification is operator-paid while claiming is sybil-cheap, so the queue itself is the attack surface; (iii) *the hole verification cannot see*: overfitting to public seeds passes tier-1 **and** tier-2 by construction, and the local search loop optimizes public seeds even in a fully honest community — the entire self-report-then-verify frame addresses honesty, while the dominant corruption channel is generality. "Enough for M2a" only if M2a's boards are explicitly labeled provisional, default views are tier-gated, and nobody treats M2a rankings as meaning anything.

### What's missing from the discussion entirely

1. **A late-game mechanism** (§2.5): deterministic prefix-replay springboards, staged objectives, or any evidence/board representation of "play from depth" — without one, the north star is decorative. (Go-Explore is the direct precedent; the design's own determinism makes it unusually cheap.)
2. **Seed-set governance** (§4.3): rotation, sizes, public/held-out ratio, what boards rank on, what selection is allowed to see. This single decision couples the Goodhart hole, the shakeup risk, and the search signal's meaning.
3. **Retention/churn as a first-class design input** (§2.2, §4.1): islands die with laptops; two-thirds of volunteers never return; the archive-at-the-edge (stepping stones) evaporates. The commons keeps elites only.
4. **A merge operator** for divergent forks (§3.2) — the actual mechanism of compound transfer once specialization begins.
5. **Evaluation economics**: who pays for canonical full-catalog runs and tier-2/3 workers; what the per-claim verification cost is; what happens to the queue under load.
6. **Credit semantics for non-code contributions** (§4.4): evaluation-only runs, influence-only ideas, and firsts under generalist bulk sweeps — the three places the ledger will be disputed first.
7. **Monoculture exposure of the single root**: every lineage inherits AutoAscend's architecture and its blind spots (no spellcasting, no late game); the map measures progress *within* that basin. A second root is rightly deferred — but the analysis should acknowledge the whole system currently explores one bot's neighborhood, and the herding dynamics of §2.1 deepen that basin rather than escape it.
