# Progress Measurement, Objectives, and the Search Archive: A First-Principles Analysis

- **Date:** 2026-08-09
- **Status:** Analysis before design. Deliberately *not* a spec — it derives requirements, maps tradeoffs, and lays out the space of viable structures, then stress-tests the tentative model from our discussion.
- **Question:** What must NetHackers' measurement + objective + archive structure actually *achieve*? What are the core tradeoffs? What structures satisfy them — and is "identity × progress-milestone as a collectively-filled MAP" theoretically sound, forced, or a category error?
- **Method:** Independent first-principles derivation, then web research of the QD / multi-task / open-endedness / benchmark-design literature and NetHack-specific prior art (NeurIPS 2021 Challenge, TNNT/devnull, BALROG lineage). Builds on, and in places argues against, [`2026-08-08-map-elites-objectives-analysis.md`](2026-08-08-map-elites-objectives-analysis.md) and the facts collected in [`2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md`](2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md).

---

## 0. Executive summary

1. **The five conflated axes are really seven jobs.** North star, search signal, progress tracking, competition, and coverage are distinct — but the list omits the two jobs that decide the architecture: **diversity maintenance** (the only job QD descriptors actually do) and **trust/verification** (which evidence counts where). Several "conflicts" dissolve once you notice two pairs are the same object at different temporal semantics: coverage is the spatial marginal of progress tracking, and per-cell competition *is* the community's selection operator — because contributors pull parents from whatever the public surfaces rank, **leaderboards and maps are not displays of the search; they are part of the search algorithm.** That feedback loop is the deepest reason the "one map" instinct is both attractive and dangerous.

2. **"Identity × progress" is half right, and the halves have different statuses.** *Identity as an archive axis is not a category error* — it is precisely the **task descriptor** of Multi-task MAP-Elites ([Mouret & Maguire 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04407)), which explicitly *replaces* behavioral descriptors with task descriptors "chosen beforehand instead of being extracted from the evaluations." But that legitimation changes what the object *is*: a multi-task archive is an **optimization portfolio** (best-per-task + cross-task transfer), not an **illumination archive** (diversity of behaviors), and it carries none of MAP-Elites' stepping-stone guarantees *within* a task. *Progress-milestone as the second axis is the forced half*: progression is (quantized, calibrated) **fitness**, so "identity × progress" is **(task × fitness)** — a structure MAP-Elites theory does not endorse as a descriptor space. It admits three coherent readings — fitness-stratified hall-of-fame (weak precedent: [FUSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610126)), append-only **attainment tracker** (strong precedent: TNNT's trophy case), or **goal-conditioned sub-tasks** (strong precedent: the Challenge report's own recommendation of "checkpointing of achievements") — and the design must pick one, because their replacement, selection, and credit semantics differ. The real error is not any single reading; it is shipping the conflation.

3. **"Objective = a named batch of characters" is the right *grading* primitive and the wrong *organizing* primitive.** Decomposed: task atoms (identity × seed × horizon) are what you **measure**; objectives are declared **functionals** (weighting + aggregation + protocol) over atoms that you **grade and rank**; the archive should be filled by **evidence atoms, not by objective names**. "What fills the map" ≠ "what you optimized." One sentence carries most of this report: **measure atoms, grade functionals, track attainment, pool elites.**

4. **Prior art is unusually convergent.** The 2021 Challenge documents exactly the failure the design must avoid (score farming: agents "optimised in-game score at the expense of progressing into the dungeon", [Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)) and recommends milestone checkpointing; TNNT abandoned unified scoring after three years and thrives on many boards + a literal per-identity milestone trophy grid ([tnnt.org](https://tnnt.org/faq)); multi-task QD and POET both validate "global gene pool + import-and-re-evaluate" as the transfer mechanism that beats independent per-task optimization ([MT-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04407), [POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)); and the leaderboard-overfitting literature ([Blum & Hardt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)) plus noisy-QD literature ([Flageat & Cully 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463)) warn that a self-reported best-ever ratchet on fixed seeds *will* inflate unless verification gates the record.

5. **Verdict on the tentative model** (§7): global gene pool + import-and-re-evaluate is the strongest element (direct MT-ME/POET support); identity-as-atom-and-objective is sound but under-typed; the board set is a sound instinct with unexamined aggregation choices; "one identity×progress MAP" is sound as a *display* and as a *tracker*, forced as an *archive*, and becomes a category error only if cell semantics are inherited from MAP-Elites without noticing the object is (task × fitness). The cleaner decomposition is four objects, not one: evidence atoms → objective functionals → an append-only attainment matrix (the public 73×rungs surface) → per-identity elite pools (the gene pool). The "MAP" survives as the front-end that renders (attainment ∪ elites); the MAP-*Elites* vocabulary is what should be scoped or dropped.

## 1. What the structure must achieve: from the physics of the problem to the jobs to be done

### 1.1 The invariant facts (constraints, not choices)

Everything below follows from seven facts about the problem that no design can change:

- **F1 — The win condition carries no gradient.** Ascension probability for current bots is ≈ 0 (no agent ascended in >500k evaluation games at the Challenge; best median score "just above Beginner" — [Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)). Any structure that measures only the north star measures nothing for years.
- **F2 — Identity is assigned, not chosen.** The game (or the evaluator) picks role/race/alignment/gender before the first action. A bot cannot steer *which* of the 73 identities it plays; it can only respond to the one it got. Identity is therefore **context/task**, never behavior.
- **F3 — One program plays every identity.** The `ArenaBot` interface is character-agnostic: a symbolic bot reads its own stats and adapts. Consequences: (a) any evaluation can emit **per-identity measurements of a single artifact**; (b) specialists **compose** — since the program observes its identity at reset, a trivial meta-program can dispatch to 73 specialists, so per-identity progress converts into random-draw progress almost for free (§5, T2). Per-identity structure is not just measurement-convenient; it is *solution-shaped*.
- **F4 — Determinism relocates noise into overfitting.** Deterministic bots on seeded NLE make any fixed seed set an *exact* measurement of itself and a *biased, overfittable* estimate of the distribution. The classical noisy-evaluation problem (archive inflation by lucky runs — [Flageat & Cully 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463)) is traded for the adaptive-overfitting problem (climbing the public seed set — [Blum & Hardt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)). Both attack a best-ever ratchet; only the countermeasures differ.
- **F5 — Contributors are self-interested and self-reporting.** The hub aggregates claims it did not produce. Measurement design *is* incentive design; every board is a target and will be Goodharted exactly as far as it is worth Goodharting ([Manheim & Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585); [DeepMind, Specification gaming](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/)).
- **F6 — The objective space is combinatorial; the atom space is small.** Any of 2⁷³−1 identity subsets × seed sets × horizons × (later) conducts is a legal "objective." But all of them are built from ≤73 identity atoms × a governed seed protocol. Structures keyed to *objectives* are unbounded; structures keyed to *atoms* are finite and stable.
- **F7 — There is a 25-year-old culture with strong revealed preferences.** Milestone ladders (devnull's star trophies), per-identity completion collecting (TNNT's Great/Lesser trophies, "NetHack Master" = all 73 combos), many parallel boards after a failed unified score, streaks as the reliability metric, celebrated failure ("Most Unique Deaths") ([community insights](2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md), [tnnt.org/leaderboards](https://tnnt.org/leaderboards)). A structure that fights this culture pays an adoption tax for no analytical gain.

### 1.2 Seven jobs, not five

The prompt's five axes — north star, search signal, progress tracking, competition, coverage — are real, but the list is incomplete and one entry is mislabeled. Enumerating the *jobs the structure must do*:

| # | Job | What it must deliver | Natural owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | **Win condition** | An unambiguous, pre-registered definition of "solved" (see §1.4 — there are at least five) | governance decision, not a measurement |
| J2 | **Search gradient** (fitness) | Dense, low-noise, cheap, monotone-*enough* w.r.t. J1 signal for selection | calibrated progression metric |
| J3 | **Diversity maintenance** | Keep mediocre-but-different lineages alive against takeover (the actual QD job) | descriptors/niches — mostly *local* |
| J4 | **Coordination / coverage** | Make visible where work is missing; deduplicate effort without assigning it | the public frontier surface |
| J5 | **Progress epistemics** | An honest, monotone, hard-to-corrupt record of how far the community has gotten, and its velocity | attainment record + trend |
| J6 | **Social reward** | Rankings, firsts, credit, impact — the fuel of a volunteer system | boards + lineage credit |
| J7 | **Trust / verification** | Decide which claims may enter which structures (cross-cutting) | evidence tiers |

Mapping the prompt's five: north star→J1, search signal→J2, progress tracking→J5, competition→J6, coverage→J4. The two omissions matter: **J3 is the only job that behavioral descriptors do** — so any argument "we need a MAP for X" where X ≠ J3 is borrowing QD authority for a non-QD job — and **J7 is the job that decides whether a collectively-filled anything is worth trusting at all.**

### 1.3 Which jobs genuinely conflict, and which are the same thing in different hats

**Genuine conflicts** (cannot be defined away; must be traded off — expanded in §5):

- **J2 vs J1 (proxy vs goal).** Any dense proxy diverges from the true goal somewhere, and a competitive population will find the divergence (score farming at the Challenge; the CoastRunners boat; Motif's agent that eats a yellow mold to *hallucinate* the oracle rather than find it — [Klissarov et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00166)). Milestone-calibrated progression is much better aligned than score but is still a proxy whose calibration (human win-probability given depth/XL, [BALROG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543)) was fit to *yesterday's* population of players and will drift as bots become unlike that population.
- **J6 vs J4 (ranking gravity vs coverage).** Boards concentrate attention on whatever they rank; a single headline board concentrates it on the easiest-to-advance identities (everyone farms Valkyrie). Coverage mechanisms (firsts, Z-score-style diminishing returns, coverage boards) exist precisely to push against ranking gravity — they are counter-incentives by construction, so J6 and J4 cannot be served by the same single number. TNNT's 16-board equilibrium is the community's discovered solution ([tnnt.org/leaderboards](https://tnnt.org/leaderboards)).
- **J5 vs J6 (ratchet vs re-ranking).** Progress epistemics wants an **append-only, community-level, best-ever** record (monotone by construction: once a Healer has verifiably reached the Castle, that fact never un-happens). Competition wants a **displaceable, solution-level, current** ranking. These are different temporal semantics; one table cannot have both without ambiguity about what a cell means.
- **J5/J6 vs J7 (speed vs trust).** Self-reported evidence makes surfaces live; a best-ever ratchet built on unverified claims is permanently corruptible by one bug or one liar. The noisy-QD result is the exact formal analogue: archives systematically *overestimate* their own quality when evaluations are unreliable ([Flageat & Cully 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463), [deep grids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14253)).
- **J3 vs J4/J5 (weird axes vs legible story).** Search-useful diversity axes (death-cause category, branch order, resource posture) are illegible to spectators; the legible public axis (milestones) is nearly useless for J3 (§2.4). One grid cannot serve both masters well.

**Same thing, different hats** (apparent conflicts that dissolve):

- **Coverage is progress-tracking's spatial marginal.** "How much of the identity×milestone matrix is attained" *is* the coverage number *and* the progress number. One object (the attainment matrix), two summaries (row-wise = per-identity progress; global fill = coverage; rightmost column = the north star). No conflict — this is the strongest argument that a single public matrix is genuinely load-bearing.
- **North star and progress tracking are one axis at two resolutions.** Ascension is the top rung of the same ladder progression is calibrated on. Choosing the metric chooses the map's columns — already recognized in the design spec, and correct.
- **Per-cell competition is selection pressure wearing a leaderboard costume.** In a distributed evolution where contributors choose parents by looking at public surfaces, **whatever the hub ranks becomes the de facto selection operator** of the community-level algorithm. This has a sharp consequence: the question "is the public map an archive or a display?" has an *empirical* answer — it functions as an archive whether or not you call it one, because it feeds parent selection. Its replacement and credit semantics must therefore be designed with QD-grade care even if it is officially "just a view" (§7.1).

### 1.4 The unpriced decision: what does "solved" mean?

"Solve NetHack" is at least five different propositions, and they imply different structures:

| Definition | Structure it privileges |
|---|---|
| D1: ascend once, any identity | a single frontier cell; pure exploration; firsts |
| D2: ascend reliably (rate ≥ x%), some identity | per-identity rate boards; **streaks** (the community's native reliability statistic) |
| D3: ascend every identity at least once | the 73-row attainment matrix; coverage incentives |
| D4: ascend reliably on all 73 | full matrix + rate columns; macro/min aggregation |
| D5: ascend reliably under the natural random draw | distribution-weighted boards; generalist (or dispatch-merged) solutions |

The tentative model quietly commits to a **dual north star** — D3/D4 via the coverage board and D5 via the `random` board. That is a defensible choice (and F3's dispatch-composability makes D5 nearly a corollary of D4), but it is a *choice*, and it should be made explicitly, because credit design differs: under D5-only, the person who staples 73 specialists together wins the headline board with other people's work; under D3/D4, credit naturally flows to cell owners and the stapler is a lineage node (§5, T2). The community precedent for pinning D-definitions is speedrunning's category system: no one asks which category is "the real game"; they ask that each category's rules be frozen and its records be per-category ([speedrun.com/nethack](https://www.speedrun.com/nethack); seeded vs unseeded segregation per [community insights §6](2026-08-08-nethack-community-insights.md)).

## 2. Fitness vs. behavioral descriptor vs. task: type-checking the map

This is the sharpest question posed, so it gets the most careful treatment. Set up the types explicitly.

- A **genome** `g`: program source (a patch lineage over AutoAscend).
- A **task** `θ = (identity, seed, horizon, ruleset)`: chosen by the evaluator *before* the episode. (Note: seeds and horizon are task coordinates too — identity is merely the *visible* one. The objective digest already encodes this; the map usually forgets it.)
- An **outcome** `o(g, θ)`: deterministic given both (F4).
- A **measurement** `m(o) ∈ [0,1]`: calibrated progression of the episode; milestones are quantizations of `m`.
- **Fitness under an objective** `F_O(g) = A( { m(o(g,θ)) : θ ∈ protocol(O) } )` for an aggregation `A`.
- A **behavioral descriptor** `b(g)` (possibly `b_O(g)`): a statistic *extracted from the episodes* intended to capture *how* the program plays, chosen to be as fitness-orthogonal as feasible.

### 2.1 Identity: task descriptor, and that is fine — but know what you bought

In vanilla MAP-Elites the archive is indexed by dimensions of variation *of solutions* — the descriptor is a function of the evaluated individual's phenotype ([Mouret & Clune 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909)). Identity fails that test trivially: it is an input, not an extract (F2). So *as vanilla MAP-Elites*, "identity as descriptor" is indeed a category error.

But the literature contains the exact construct needed, and it is not a stretch — it is the founding move of **Multi-task MAP-Elites**: "each niche corresponds to a task… the rest of the algorithm is the same as the vanilla MAP-Elites except that *behavioral descriptors are replaced by task descriptors, which are chosen beforehand instead of being extracted from the evaluations*" ([Mouret & Maguire 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04407)). Selection is uniform across the whole archive; a child is evaluated on a task chosen near its parent's task (a bandit-tuned tournament); competition happens strictly within a task's cell. On 2,000–5,000-task benchmarks this **beat independent per-task CMA-ES at equal budget**, with the stated mechanism: "a high-performing solution for a task is likely to be a good starting point to solve another task of the same family" — the elites of related tasks cluster in genotype space (the "elite hypervolume"). Successors refine the same separation: behavior descriptors *within* each task ([MT-MB-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01264)), continuous task spaces ([PT-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01275)).

NetHackers is a textbook instance: 73 tasks, one genome representation, strongly correlated task difficulty (the Challenge found the same role ordering for every agent — Barbarian/Monk/Samurai/Valkyrie easy, Healer/Rogue/Tourist/Wizard hard — [Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)), and cheap cross-task re-evaluation (F3, F4). **So: identity as the archive's cell axis is theoretically licensed.**

What the license does *not* grant: a multi-task archive is a different animal from an illumination archive. It holds **one elite per task** and preserves **no within-task diversity**; its search value comes *entirely* from cross-task transfer, not from stepping-stone preservation. It is best understood as an **optimization portfolio with a transfer bus**, not as "illuminating a behavior space." Three consequences:

1. The QD-score/illumination rhetoric ("the map illuminates how quality varies across kinds of solutions") does not apply — the map illuminates how *task difficulty* varies, which is interesting but is a property of NetHack, not of the solutions.
2. Deception *within* a task is unaddressed: if the best path to ascending a Wizard passes through code that temporarily scores worse on Wizard, one-elite-per-identity will delete it. That job (J3) must live somewhere else — realistically in contributors' local archives with real behavioral descriptors, exactly as the current design has it; MT-MB-ME is the published upgrade path if hub-side takeover is observed.
3. One-elite-per-task plus a shared global pool has a **herding failure mode** with no analogue in single-machine MT-ME: if every contributor imports the same top elites, the community's effective population collapses toward one lineage (takeover). MT-ME's uniform-over-cells selection is itself the mitigation hint — parent sampling should be spread over cells, not concentrated on the global best (§5, T3).

### 2.2 Progression: fitness — and milestones are quantized fitness

The progression metric is *definitionally* the fitness: it is what selection maximizes, calibrated to ascension probability ([BALROG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543) maps deepest dungeon level (1–50) and experience level (1–30) to human win-probability from the [NLD corpus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00539); the design's ladder anchors the same object to community-validated milestones — devnull's star ladder ∪ TNNT's achievements). The milestone ladder and the fitness are one object at two resolutions — the design spec says this correctly ("choosing the metric locks the columns").

Therefore "identity × progress-rung" is, type-wise, **(task × quantized-fitness)**. The question is whether task × fitness is a legitimate archive index. MAP-Elites theory says: not as a *behavioral* dimension — a descriptor that is monotone in fitness collapses the archive into a fitness histogram and preserves no fitness-orthogonal variation. But there are three coherent readings of the structure, and they are *different objects with different rules*:

### 2.3 Three readings of the progress columns — pick one, because the semantics differ

**Reading A — fitness-stratified archive ("hall of fame per rung").** Descriptor = max-rung-reached on the seed set (deterministic under F4); fitness within cell = mean progression. Steelman: max and mean are *different functionals* of the same episodes, and their disagreement is genuinely informative — a consistent grinder (high mean, shallow max) and a volatile diver (low mean, deep max) land in different cells, and the diver is exactly the classic stepping stone a mean-greedy ladder deletes. Precedent for keeping diversity along the fitness axis exists (fitness-uniform selection, [Hutter & Legg 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610126)) and GigaEvo itself ships a fitness×validity space. Weaknesses: (i) both axes are still *outcome statistics* — two programs in the same cell can play identically-scoring but mechanistically alien games, and two mechanistic twins can straddle cells, so the cells only weakly track the code diversity that actually matters for search (§2.4); (ii) sub-frontier cells mostly fill with dominated near-clones of frontier elites, which the public surface then *celebrates* — an odd look for J5/J6; (iii) replacement is ambiguous the moment a solution's max-rung improves (it *moves cells*, orphaning its old cell — archives with fitness-coupled descriptors churn by construction).

**Reading B — append-only attainment matrix (a tracker, not an archive).** Cell (I, R) = "has any solution, at evidence tier ≥ t, ever gotten identity I to rung R (+ who first, + best supporting stats)". No replacement semantics, no parent-selection semantics — an existence record. This is *literally* TNNT's trophy structure ported to bots: the Lesser Race/Role trophies are "complete Mines + Sokoban with every combo" — an intermediate-milestone × identity grid — and "NetHack Master" is all-73-combos ascended ([tnnt.org/trophies](https://tnnt.org/trophies), [NetHack Wiki: TNNT](https://nethackwiki.com/wiki/The_November_NetHack_Tournament)). Two properties make this the *most trustworthy* public object available: **monotonicity** (existence claims never regress, so the ratchet is semantically honest) and **cheap verifiability** (an attainment claim is a single episode whose action trace replays deterministically — tier-2 verification of a milestone is one replay, no statistics involved). Contrast: a *mean-score* claim is a statistical assertion over a seed set, attackable by seed overfitting and inflatable by selective reporting — the fragile kind of claim ([Blum & Hardt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585); [Flageat & Cully 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463)). A milestone-first public record is evidence-first by construction.

**Reading C — goal-conditioned sub-tasks.** Treat (identity, milestone) as a *task pair*: "reach R as I" is an objective with its own fitness — success rate over the seed set, then speed (turns) or margin. Now the columns are tasks, not fitness, and the type error evaporates: the archive is MT-ME over a 73×~10 goal grid, and lower cells hold *non-dominated* specialists (the best-at-reliably-reaching-Sokoban program is a meaningful, reusable artifact — a robust early-game chassis — not a worse ascender). This is exactly the Challenge organizers' recommendation: "a new metric, perhaps involving the checkpointing of achievements", so "the ranking of agents better reflects progress in the game" ([Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)); it is also how the human community already thinks (devnull stars are *per-game goals*, TNNT achievements are checkpoints). Cost: a fitness definition per column, more contract surface, and the risk of premature ontology (do we *know* speed-to-Mines is the right column-2 fitness? No).

**The category-error verdict, precisely:** the error is not "identity × progress is nonsense" — readings B and C are both sound and well-precedented, and even A is defensible as a thin risk-posture stratification. The error is **shipping the map without deciding which reading it is**, because the three have incompatible answers to the three questions that define an archive: *what replaces what* (A: better mean within same max-rung; B: nothing, append-only; C: better reliability@goal), *what gets selected as parents* (A: any cell incl. dominated; B: n/a — needs a separate pool; C: goal-specialists), and *what gets credited* (A: current cell holders; B: firsts + bests; C: per-goal records). The MAP-Elites *name* smuggles in Reading-A semantics; the community's instincts (TNNT) and trust needs pull toward B; the search-theoretic ideal with budget pulls toward C-later.

### 2.4 Why milestone cells are weak *search* stepping stones here (the Go-Explore disanalogy)

The strongest intuition for progress-indexed archives comes from Go-Explore, which cracked Montezuma's Revenge with an archive of cells that are nakedly progress-correlated (x/y position, room number, *level* number, keys held) ([Ecoffet et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995)). Doesn't that legitimize milestone cells as stepping stones?

No — and the reason is load-bearing. Go-Explore's cells archive **states with restorable trajectories**: the algorithm *returns* to a cell (by simulator restore or deterministic replay) and explores onward from there. The stepping stone is the *game state*. NetHackers evaluates full programs from turn zero; nothing restores. The stepping stone is **the code**, and an intermediate-rung *outcome* is only valuable insofar as the code that produced it contains reusable mechanism (a better Sokoban solver, a prayer-budget policy). Outcome-rung is a weak proxy for mechanism novelty: most sub-frontier outcomes are produced by strictly-worse versions of frontier code, not by different ideas. This is why J3 (diversity) wants *mechanism-flavored* descriptors (death-cause taxonomy, branch order, resource posture, module-level diffs) — which are exactly the descriptors that belong in contributors' *local* archives where they can be iterated freely, not frozen into a hub contract. The hub-side archive earns its keep through MT-ME transfer across identities, not through rung-diversity within them.

(One more disanalogy worth recording: Go-Explore's "return then explore" *could* be imported deliberately — seed-specific action-prefix replays as curriculum for the mutation engine, "explore from the Castle on seed 17". That is a harness technique, not an archive structure, and is out of scope here — but it shows where the Go-Explore intuition actually pays off in this project.)

## 3. Decomposing "objective": atoms, functionals, protocols

"Objective = a named batch of characters to grade on" bundles five roles: (i) a **task weighting** (which identities, with what weights), (ii) an **aggregation** (how episodes collapse to a score), (iii) an **evaluation protocol** (seed set, horizon, budget — the part that decides comparability), (iv) an implied **archive-filling** role (what your run contributes to the shared structure), and (v) an implied **board** (where you rank). The bundle works as a UX ("I'm optimizing `role:valkyrie`") but hides the cleaner algebra:

- **Task atom:** `(identity, seed-protocol, horizon)`. Finite, stable, exhaustive. What gets *measured*.
- **Objective:** `(weighting w over atoms, aggregation A, protocol P)` + a digest. What gets *graded*. `random` = natural-draw weights (1/26…1/208 — NetHack picks role first, so the draw is far from uniform over the 73); `all` = uniform (or role-macro) weights; `role:val` = an indicator weighting; a single identity = a point mass. Scores compare only within a digest — the existing rule, and correct.
- **Board:** objective + evidence-tier filter + ranking keys. What gets *ranked*.
- **Archive/pool:** filled by **evidence atoms**, independent of which named objective the contributor happened to declare. A run graded on `role:val` still produced per-identity, per-seed episodes; those atoms update Valkyrie rows regardless. **Objectives don't fill maps; evidence does.** (The current design's full-catalog canonical suite already embodies this — every verified candidate arrives with all-73 measurements.)
- **Progress:** functionals over the community *frontier* (per-atom best-ever at tier ≥ t), not over any single solution.

Two under-decided components surface immediately once the bundle is split:

**The aggregation menu is a real design axis, with literature.** Options, each with a documented failure mode: *natural-weighted mean* (= `random`; dominated by common identities; hides rare-identity failure); *uniform mean over 73* (overweights roles with many legal combos — Wizard has 10 identities, Valkyrie 3; "uniform over what?" is itself a choice); *macro-role mean* (equal role weight — the old hub's choice); *median* (ignores the tails entirely — a suite median is unchanged even if scores on nearly half the identities collapse to zero, the exact critique from [Agarwal et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)); *IQM + stratified bootstrap CIs + performance profiles* (the statistically defensible default for aggregate claims, ibid.); *min / lower-quantile* (the robustness-honest statistic; brutal but ungameable); *geometric mean over milestone/identity success rates* (Crafter's `S = exp(mean ln(1+sᵢ)) − 1`, chosen precisely so that "collecting a diamond 1% of the time instead of 0% is a meaningful improvement, whereas collecting wood 95% instead of 90% is not" — [Hafner 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06780)). The geometric mean deserves special attention: it is a **coverage incentive packaged inside a single number** — an alternative (or complement) to a separate coverage board. *Lexicographic (ascensions, then progression)* is the Challenge's own shape — its ranking tuple was "number of ascensions, median in-game score, and mean in-game score" ([Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889)) — and is the natural end-state for when ascensions stop being identically zero: the proxy should be the tiebreaker under the true goal, never the reverse.

**`random` is a functional, not a protocol, until you decide otherwise.** Forced-identity episodes and random-`@` episodes are different RNG protocols (the draw consumes RNG state), so a "random" board can be (a) *measured* under a genuine random-draw protocol — its own digest, never merged — or (b) *estimated* as the natural-weighted rollup of per-identity atoms, labeled as such. Both are legitimate; silently conflating them is not. (The prior analysis got this right; it is restated here because the tentative model's "boards = `random` + coverage" leaves it implicit.)

**Objectives-as-batches also hide the seed governance question.** Fixed public seeds + self-reported bests = a public leaderboard in the [Blum & Hardt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585) adaptive regime: their boosting-attack analysis shows a public holdout is *provably* farmable by repeated queries, and their fix — release a new number only when improvement exceeds a significance threshold — transfers directly (a map/board ratchet should ignore sub-threshold "improvements" on public seeds; Kaggle's public/private split ≈ the tier-1/tier-3 ladder already in the design). Seed-set *rotation* fights overfitting but breaks the monotone record — a genuine dilemma (§5, T4).

## 4. Prior art, organized by what it teaches

### 4.1 The NeurIPS 2021 NetHack Challenge: what one scalar over a random draw buys you

Structure: `NetHackChallenge-v0` (113 actions), **random role/race/alignment/gender enforced each episode**, dev-phase 512-episode rollouts, test-phase 4,096-episode rollouts; ranking tuple **ascensions → median score → mean score**; 1M-step / 30-min caps ([report](https://nethackchallenge.com/report.html), [Hambro et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11889), [AIcrowd rules](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/neurips-2021-the-nethack-challenge/challenge_rules)).

Documented pathologies, in the organizers' own words: teams "**optimised in-game score at the expense of progressing into the dungeon for ascension**", including "**early termination of episodes beyond a certain internal score**" and "**intentional restriction of the agent to the top-level dungeons**" — score farming as predicted by every Goodhart taxonomy. No agent ascended; the winner's median 5,300 is "just above a Beginner score." Their forward recommendation is the single most direct piece of prior art for this design: refine metrics "**such that the ranking of agents better reflects progress in the game**", suggesting "**a new metric, perhaps involving the checkpointing of achievements**" — i.e., the organizers of the only bot competition in this domain, post-mortem, endorse milestone checkpointing (Reading B/C of §2.3) over score.

Also instructive is what the random-draw design *hid*: per-role breakdowns had to be excavated afterward (easy roles ~consistently outscored hard ones for every agent), and a 1-in-20 Valkyrie tail drove headline numbers. A distribution-weighted scalar grades the *portfolio* while concealing the *per-task structure* — acceptable for a one-shot contest, hostile to a multi-year coordination effort that needs to know *which* tasks are unsolved.

### 4.2 TNNT / devnull / Junethack: 25 years of measurement design by iteration

- **Unified scoring died on contact with the community.** TNNT ran a composite score for three years and abandoned it in 2021 — "biases in the scoring algorithm… no objective score on what was ultimately a subjective measure" — in favor of **16 parallel leaderboards** (Most Ascensions, Lowest Turncount, Most Conducts in One Ascension, Longest Streak, Most Unique Deaths, Highest Z-score, …) ([tnnt.org/faq](https://tnnt.org/faq), [tnnt.org/leaderboards](https://tnnt.org/leaderboards), [NetHack Wiki: TNNT](https://nethackwiki.com/wiki/The_November_NetHack_Tournament)). The general lesson: when a community's goals are plural, a unified scalar is not a simplification, it is a fight.
- **The per-identity milestone grid already exists — as trophies, not as an archive.** Great/Lesser Race and Role trophies (Lesser = complete Mines' End + Sokoban with every combo of a race/role — an explicit *intermediate-milestone × identity* structure), "NetHack Master" (ascend all 73 starting combos), achievements (325+ tracked in-game) ([tnnt.org/trophies](https://tnnt.org/trophies)). Devnull's star ladder is a community-validated total order on milestones (Sokoban → Mines' End → nemesis → Medusa → Castle → Gehennom → Vlad → Rodney → invocation → Amulet → Planes → Astral → ascension) ([devnull tournament](https://nethack.fandom.com/wiki//dev/null/nethack_tournament)). Crucially, these grids are **attainment records** — append-only, existence-based, first-holder-credited — never "archives" with replacement semantics. The human community converged on Reading B.
- **Coverage has a native metric with diminishing returns built in**: Z-score (1 + ½ + ⅓ + … per repeated ascension of the same role) — 13 unique-role wins = 13.0 but 13 Valkyries ≈ 3.18 ([tnnt.org/faq](https://tnnt.org/faq)) — the community's own answer to leaderboard gravity, structurally identical in intent to Crafter's geometric mean.
- **Reliability has a native metric**: streaks (consecutive ascensions), the premier human skill signal — a direct template for D2/D4-style "reliably" claims (consecutive-seed ascensions).
- **Bots are banned everywhere** in human tournaments — the niche NetHackers occupies is genuinely open, and the trophy/board grammar is free to reuse.

### 4.3 The score→progression lineage, and why even progression must be held loosely

NetHack score is community-distrusted (pudding-farm era; "low score is prestigious"), was the Challenge's documented failure mode (§4.1), and has motivated three successive replacements: exploration-based "scout" metrics, and BALROG's **data-informed progression** — deepest dungeon level × experience level mapped to empirical human win-probability, 0–100 ([BALROG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543), [docs](https://balrog-ai.github.io/docs/envs/nle.html), discussion in [Revisiting the NLE](https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2026/blog/2026/revisiting-the-nle/)). (There is no public `nle-progress` package as of this writing — the term denotes this metric lineage; the project's concrete implementation is the sibling repo's `progress.py`.)

Three cautions from adjacent work:

1. **Calibration is population-relative.** BALROG's curve answers "what fraction of *humans in the NLD corpus* who reached (depth, XL) went on to win" — a prior fit to a population today's bots do not resemble and tomorrow's bots will resemble even less. As the frontier moves, the metric's *ordering* between, say, deep-but-fragile and shallow-but-kitted states can invert relative to true ascension probability. This argues for milestone anchors (which are facts about the game, not about a population) over probability calibration at the *record* level, keeping the calibrated scalar for *selection* where its density earns its keep — and for treating recalibration as versioned governance (a metric change forks comparability like a rules change forks a speedrun category).
2. **Composite rewards misalign in ways their components don't.** Motif's agent, given a benign-looking combination of intrinsic and task reward, learned to eat a yellow mold and *hallucinate* the oracle instead of finding it — "misalignment by composition" ([Klissarov et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00166)). Every additive term in a fitness formula (crash-rate penalties, validity terms, milestone bonuses) is an attack surface; the Challenge's score farmers and CoastRunners' circling boat ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/)) are the same phenomenon at different levels of sophistication. A taxonomy for reasoning about which variant you're exposed to: [Manheim & Garrabrant 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585).
3. **Milestone metrics plateau between milestones.** A reviewer critique of BALROG's metric — principled "but too sparse to be used as a reward signal" ([literature review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/balrog-benchmarking-agentic-llm-and-vlm-reasoning-on-games)) — matters less for episode-level evolutionary selection than for RL, but it re-emerges at frontier gaps: when the whole population is stuck between Castle and Gehennom, the ladder is locally flat and selection has nothing to grip. Mitigations are within-rung tiebreakers (turns, kit completeness, survival margin), which is quietly the strongest argument for Reading C's per-column fitness.

### 4.4 QD and multi-task theory: what each construct is *for*

Compressed, since §2 does the application: MAP-Elites = illumination of a *behavior* space; descriptor must be fitness-orthogonal variation of solutions ([Mouret & Clune 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909); QD framing and QD-score in [Pugh et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2016.00040/full)); CVT-MAP-Elites for higher-dim descriptor spaces ([Vassiliades et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05729)); MT-ME = task-descriptor archive, one elite per task, global selection, beats per-task optimization ([Mouret & Maguire 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04407)); MT-MB-ME = small behavior archive per task when one-elite-per-task is too greedy ([Anne & Mouret 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01264)); PT-ME for continuous task spaces ([2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01275)); noisy/uncertain QD: best-ever archives **overestimate themselves under unreliable evaluation**, and corrected ("ground-truth re-evaluated") metrics + re-evaluation schemes are the fix ([Flageat & Cully 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463); [deep-grid ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14253)) — under F4 this maps to seed-overfitting + self-report bugs rather than sampling noise, but the archive-inflation conclusion transfers intact.

### 4.5 Open-endedness: transfer and rank-based comparison

- **POET** is the strongest independent validation of import-and-re-evaluate: environment-agent pairs (environments = niches/tasks), with periodic transfer attempts where a candidate replaces a target's incumbent **only if it beats it after (re-)evaluation on the target environment** — and transfer is not an optimization: it is *the* mechanism. Environments POET solved score 230+ where direct optimization from scratch reaches 13.6–39.6, and a hand-built direct-path curriculum also fails; "in POET without transfer, no extremely challenging environments are solved at all" ([Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)). Prediction for NetHackers: the hard identities (Tourist, Healer, Wizard) will most likely *not* fall to direct per-identity grinding, but to code bred on adjacent identities and imported — the structure should make that path frictionless and *credited*.
- **Enhanced POET** contributes two measurement ideas: **PATA-EC** — when raw scores across tasks are incomparable, compare *rankings induced over a shared population* (clip → rank → normalize) — a principled alternative any time the design is tempted to compare across identities ("which identity is most improvable?" can be answered in rank space without ever comparing progression values); and **ANNECS** — count of novel challenges created-and-solved over time — the precedent for measuring *community velocity* as **first-time cell attainments per epoch** rather than as movement of any scalar ([Wang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08536)).
- **Curriculum/task-selection**: automatic curriculum learning selects tasks by *learning progress* — work where improvement is fastest ([Portelas et al. 2020 survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04664); ALP-GMM [1910.07224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07224)); Prioritized Level Replay selects *seeds* by learning potential ([Jiang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03934)). Both are the principled version of "the map suggests where to work" — as *suggestion* (a per-cell recent-velocity signal on the map), never assignment (thin-hub principle).

### 4.6 Distributed community search: the closest living systems

- **Fishtest** (Stockfish) is the nearest real-world relative: volunteer-distributed evaluation of program patches against a single trunk, with acceptance gated by sequential statistics (SPRT on Elo) at two time controls ([fishtest](https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest)). Its lessons cut both ways: (a) a community *can* sustain years of massive distributed program evolution when the measurement is trusted and the credit is visible; (b) Fishtest needs **no map at all because chess is one task** — NetHackers' 73-task structure is exactly what forces a matrix where Stockfish needs a single queue; (c) Fishtest *centralizes* evaluation to get trust, which NetHackers' edge-compute model refuses — hence the evidence-tier ladder is not optional bureaucracy, it is the substitute for Fishtest's central referee.
- **LLM-driven evolution precedents**: ELM (LLM mutation + MAP-Elites over code, [Lehman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08896)), FunSearch (islands, best-programs-in-prompt, [Romera-Paredes et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6)), AlphaEvolve (MAP-Elites + islands hybrid database, [DeepMind 2025](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)) — all validate archive/island memory for LLM mutation engines; none faces the multi-task or the trust problem, which is where this design's novelty actually lies.
- **Leaderboard integrity**: public/private splits (Kaggle) and the Ladder mechanism ([Blum & Hardt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)) for adaptive overfitting; redundant-computation quorums (BOINC) and replay-based verification (speedrunning) as the two classical trust models for self-reported results — the design's deterministic tier-2 replay is the cheap, superior domain-specific form of the latter.

## 5. The core tradeoffs

Each stated as the two goods in tension, with what structure can and cannot buy.

**T1 — Dense proxy vs. incorruptible goal (J2 vs J1).** Density is non-negotiable (F1) and every dense signal is corruptible (§4.3). Structure can: keep the proxy *out* of the record layer (records = milestone facts + ascensions; proxy = selection-layer tiebreak), make the ranking lexicographic with the true goal on top (Challenge precedent), and version the metric like a ruleset. Structure cannot: make any fixed proxy stay aligned at a moving frontier — periodic recalibration is governance, not math.

**T2 — Specialists vs. generalists (per-task vs distributional).** F3's dispatch-composability makes these *convergent in the artifact* (73 specialists staple into one random-draw player) but *divergent in credit and in engineering*: a stapled meta-bot wins D5 boards with other people's cells (credit must flow through lineage to cell owners, or stapling becomes the dominant, corrosive strategy); and 73 diverging forks of a shared root trade transfer-leverage (MT-ME's elite hypervolume — elites of related tasks are *similar*) for per-task fit, with real maintenance cost. The structure can price this (impact = lineage-weighted; boards distinguish "composed" solutions) but not remove it.

**T3 — One-elite-per-task vs. diversity (takeover risk).** MT-ME's greedy cells are the right *hub* economics, but a *global* pool everyone imports from adds herding: distributed contributors defaulting to "import the top elite" collapse the community's effective diversity — the island-model benefit (semi-isolation) is destroyed by too-eager migration, the oldest result in parallel EC. Mitigations live in *pull policy*, not hub state: randomized/cell-spread parent sampling (MT-ME selects uniformly over *all* cells, not the best), top-*k* per cell rather than top-1, and local archives as the diversity reservoir. MT-MB-ME is the structural upgrade if takeover is observed empirically.

**T4 — Monotone record vs. honest record (ratchet vs re-evaluation).** J5 wants append-only; F4/F5 make unverified appends inflationary (lucky-seed analogue: overfit-seed), and noisy-QD says the archive will flatter itself ([2302.00463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463)). The reconciliation is that **only existence claims ratchet cleanly** (milestone attainments, replay-verified — Reading B), while **statistical claims must stay displaceable and tiered** (means, rates — boards, not records). Seed rotation vs. frozen seeds remains genuinely unresolved: rotation fights overfitting but forks the record book; the speedrun-category precedent (freeze rules per category epoch, start a new board on change) is the least-bad known answer.

**T5 — Ranking gravity vs. coverage (J6 vs J4).** Whatever the headline board rewards will absorb most compute (§4.1's easy-role dominance; NAO's Valkyrie pileup). Counter-structures with precedent: firsts/named credit per cell (TNNT trophies), diminishing-returns coverage scores (Z-score; Crafter-style geometric aggregation), coverage boards. Each is itself Goodhartable — cell-sniping trivial deltas on empty cells is the obvious attack — so coverage credit needs significance margins (the Ladder's release-only-significant-improvements rule, applied socially).

**T6 — Legibility vs. structural honesty (one map vs the true object).** The true public object is: a frontier vector over 73 atoms × evidence tiers × protocol digests, plus velocity. A single 73×10 grid is a *projection* that must lie about something (tiers, protocols, or recency). The design can choose *which* lie: the least harmful is rendering tiers as visually distinct layers of one grid (already the design's instinct) and keeping digests as board separations, never grid overlays.

**T7 — Frozen comparability vs. living difficulty.** Horizon, NLE version, metric version, seed governance — every one is part of task identity (F6); changing any forks history. Structure can make forking cheap and explicit (epoch labels on records, à la speedrun categories and TNNT yearly archives) but cannot make evolution of the rules free.

## 6. The space of viable structures

Not one answer — a morphology. The load-bearing choices are nearly orthogonal; most combinations are coherent.

**Axis 1 — the hub-side elite structure (the gene pool):**
- **S1: Pure MT-ME pool.** 73 cells (one per identity; protocol-digest-namespaced), top-*k* elites per cell by (ascensions, progression); global selection; import-and-re-evaluate. Minimal, fully licensed by theory. *(k>1 is cheap takeover insurance — see T3.)*
- **S2: S1 + max-rung stratification** (Reading A columns as archive cells). Buys thin risk-posture diversity at the cost of dominated-cell clutter and churny replacement. Weakly justified; mostly harmless if k-per-cell already exists.
- **S3: S1 + goal-conditioned columns** (Reading C: cells = (identity, milestone), fitness = reliability@goal then speed). The Challenge-endorsed shape; gives sub-frontier cells non-dominated meaning; costs per-column fitness definitions. Natural *later* upgrade, once rung coverage stops moving.
- **S4: S1 + within-identity behavior descriptors at the hub** (MT-MB-ME). Only if hub-side takeover is empirically observed; otherwise premature contract surface — behavior descriptors belong local (J3).
- **S5: No reified hub archive.** Evidence pool + boards + attainment display; "the pool" is a *query* (`top-k per identity per tier`), not an object. Leanest possible; loses nothing computationally (S1 is materializable as exactly this query); loses *social objects* — cells as ownable, first-able things. Given J6's importance, S5's economics with S1's presentation is the natural synthesis: **archive as query, cells as social objects.**

**Axis 2 — the public progress object:** attainment matrix (Reading B; append-only; tier-layered; firsts credited) vs. current-elites grid (Reading A/C snapshot) vs. both layered. The attainment matrix is the only one whose ratchet is semantically honest (T4) and whose verification is single-replay cheap; the elites grid is the only one that shows *who holds* cells now. They answer different questions; both render on one 73×rungs canvas.

**Axis 3 — boards (each = objective functional + tier filter):** headline `random` (measured-protocol or labeled-estimate rollup); `all` with a *consciously chosen* aggregation (uniform-73 vs macro-role vs IQM vs geometric — §3); robustness cuts (min / lower-quantile per solution); coverage (Z-score-like or geometric, with anti-sniping margins); firsts; streaks (reliability, community-native); diagnostics-as-culture (unique deaths). Never one unified score (TNNT).

**Axis 4 — progress/velocity summaries:** matrix fill % (tiered); frontier QD-score (Σ over identities of best progression — the community "readiness" scalar, [Pugh et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2016.00040/full)); **first-attainments per epoch** (ANNECS-style velocity, [2003.08536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08536)); ascension counter. Level and velocity are different numbers; publish both.

**Axis 5 — governance:** metric/protocol epoching (T7); ratchet gates (tier thresholds + significance margins, [Ladder](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585)); seed policy (frozen public + HMAC heldout, rotation only at epoch boundaries); recalibration cadence for the progression curve.

## 7. Assessment of the tentative model

The model under discussion: **(a)** one identity×progress MAP, filled collectively; **(b)** boards = `random` + a `coverage` board, with a per-solution `all` cut; **(c)** identity doubles as a map-atom AND a valid objective; **(d)** global gene pool + import-and-re-evaluate.

### (a) "One identity×progress MAP, filled collectively" — sound spine, conflated semantics

- **Sound:** the identity axis (MT-ME task descriptor; finite, exhaustive, non-overlapping, culturally native — §2.1, §4.2); one map rather than per-objective archives (F6; the 2026-08-08 analysis's Option-D argument stands); collective filling from evidence atoms (§3 — with the crucial reframe that *evidence*, not declared objectives, fills it); the map as the shared coordination/progress surface (J4≡J5, §1.3).
- **Forced:** "×progress" *as an archive descriptor*. It is (task × quantized-fitness) — §2.2/§2.3 — and its Reading-A value (risk-posture stratification) is thin because outcome-rungs are weak proxies for code diversity (§2.4, the Go-Explore disanalogy). The lower cells of an A-style map are mostly dominated clones with ambiguous replacement rules.
- **Category error — only if unexamined:** calling it "a MAP-Elites archive" imports Reading-A semantics (cells hold displaceable elites, cells feed selection, cells earn credit) onto an object the community will read as Reading B (a trophy case) and that theory would prefer as Reading C (goal sub-tasks) later. The three readings disagree on replacement, selection, and credit (§2.3); shipping the grid without pinning the reading ships the error. And because public surfaces *are* selection operators in a distributed search (§1.3), the ambiguity would not stay cosmetic — it would quietly define the community's algorithm.
- **Cleaner resolution implied by the analysis:** keep the 73×rungs *canvas*, but let it render **two layered objects with clean types** — the **attainment matrix** (append-only, tier-layered, firsts; the ratchet-honest, single-replay-verifiable record: J4+J5+part of J6) and the **per-identity elite pool** (top-k per identity per tier, materializable as a query: J2-transfer + the rest of J6). Drop, or explicitly scope, the "MAP-Elites" name: what is collectively filled is a multi-task frontier + a trophy grid, and it is *stronger* for being named what it is. Reading C (goal-conditioned columns with reliability fitness) is the principled future upgrade once first-attainment velocity slows; S4/MT-MB-ME is the escape hatch if hub takeover appears.

### (b) "Boards = `random` + `coverage`, with per-solution `all` cut" — right instinct, unpriced choices

- **Sound:** plural boards (TNNT's hard-won lesson, §4.2); `random` as the game-as-dealt headline (Challenge continuity); a coverage counterweight (T5); a per-solution cross-identity cut (robustness display).
- **Underspecified:** (i) `random` must declare measured-protocol vs weighted-estimate (§3); (ii) the `all` cut must pick its aggregation consciously — uniform-73 vs macro-role vs IQM vs geometric are *different orderings* with different incentive fields (§3), and a min/quantile cut is arguably the more honest robustness statistic than any mean; (iii) the coverage board needs anti-sniping margins and a diminishing-returns shape (Z-score/geometric precedents) or it becomes a race to stamp trivial deltas on empty cells; (iv) missing boards with high cultural pull and real informational value: **firsts** (named credit per cell — the strongest coverage incentive of all), **streaks** (the native "reliably" operationalization, needed the moment D2/D4 matters), unique-deaths (diagnostic + on-culture). (v) The unpriced meta-decision of §1.4: `random` + `coverage` is a *dual north star* (D5 + D3/D4) — fine, but say so, and design credit so the D5 board doesn't reward stapling other people's D3 work uncredited (T2).
- **Design them as selection operators, not just displays** (§1.3): whatever these boards rank is what contributors will pull as parents. A `random` board headed by a dispatch-merge and a coverage board headed by a cell-sniper are *bad parent distributions*, not just bad optics.

### (c) "Identity doubles as a map-atom AND a valid objective" — sound, and necessarily so; but the typing is loose

Atoms *must* be valid objectives, or specialists could never be graded, and per-task competition — the one invariant every structure in this report shares — would have no home. MT-ME's whole construction presumes tasks are individually evaluable. Two tightenings: an identity alone is not yet an objective — only (identity + seed protocol + horizon + aggregation) is, and the digest discipline already knows this; and the doubling is asymmetric — every atom is an objective, but most objectives (`random`, slices, `all`) are *not* atoms, they are functionals over atoms (§3). Atoms measure; functionals grade. Keeping that asymmetry explicit is what prevents the hub from ever growing "one archive per objective" again.

### (d) "Global gene pool + import-and-re-evaluate" — the strongest element; refine the pull policy

Triple-precedented: MT-ME's global selection with within-task competition beats per-task optimization at equal budget ([2003.04407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04407)); POET's transfers-after-re-evaluation are *the* mechanism without which its hard tasks are never solved ([1901.01753](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)); Enhanced POET's both-gates refinement (direct *and* fine-tuned transfer must beat the incumbent's recent best) is the noise-hardened version worth copying into import policy ([2003.08536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08536)). Re-evaluation-before-counting also cleanly dissolves cross-identity score incomparability (nothing is ever compared across tasks — only code moves). Refinements the analysis demands: **anti-herding pull policy** (spread parent sampling across cells; top-k not top-1 — T3); **budgeted re-evaluation** (imports cost eval budget; the bandit-flavored task-tournament of MT-ME is the precedent for spending it near the contributor's task); **credit for transfer** (lineage impact is what makes exporting elites individually rational — without it the gene pool is a commons that sophisticated contributors strip-mine); and **expect the hard identities to fall via imports, not grinding** (POET's central empirical lesson — worth engineering the "import a foreign elite into your objective" path to be the *easiest* action in the system).

### Open questions analysis cannot settle (flagged for M1 empirics)

1. **Does cross-identity transfer actually pay here?** MT-ME's elite-hypervolume assumption (related tasks → similar elites) is plausible for one shared codebase but unmeasured; if per-identity elites converge to near-identical code, the map's gene-pool function is vestigial (and that would itself be fine — the attainment/credit functions stand alone).
2. **Milestone spacing at the frontier gap.** Whether the ladder is flat exactly where the population stalls (Castle→Gehennom) decides how soon Reading-C tiebreak fitness is needed.
3. **Seed-count and overfitting floor.** How fast public-seed scores diverge from heldout scores under real optimization pressure decides ratchet margins and rotation cadence (T4).
4. **Takeover diagnostics.** Whether hub-side one-elite-per-identity visibly collapses community diversity decides if MT-MB-ME (S4) is ever needed.
